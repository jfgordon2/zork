# QA checklist for each GBA screen

Use one row per room or interface screen **and per meaningful state**. For example, use separate rows for the Egyptian Room with the coffin closed, open with the sceptre, open and empty, and dropped on the floor. Record the ROM build, chapter and room ID when available, entry route, setup, actions, and a native 240 × 160 capture.

| Check | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Story match | The visible room, lighting, people, items, exits, and obstacles agree with the current story text. Recheck after actions that change the description. |
| Placement | Every visible item sits on the floor, a support, or inside its container. Character scale, posture, gaze, and contact fit the scene and their current activity. |
| Entity identity | Check the story to identify the same entity across appearances. Compare its canonical sprite and state poses at equivalent scale: head silhouette, body proportions, anatomy, markings, crest, and materials agree. Color similarity alone is insufficient, and transformations require story support. |
| Route continuity | The view makes sense from each meaningful approach. Shared landmarks, materials, water flow, and lighting carry across the route. Open passages are legible from both sides. Through a door, show the actual destination room; reverse views depict the same physical door and consistent swing. Compare shared structures across connected views, including the same opening's shape, scale, materials, supports, and alignment. For a ladder, check matching rails/rungs and that it descends through the upper room's floor and rises through the lower room's ceiling. |
| Architectural access | Trace a continuous floor or story-supported stair, slope, ladder, or bridge from the viewer to and through every traversable entrance. Thresholds meet the local floor plane, with no wall strip, unexplained drop, or disconnected landing beneath them. Bridges meet their banks. High cracks and blocked openings read as their actual story role. Open doors remain attached to a plausible hinge, clear the floor and walking route, and agree on swing direction in both room views; embedded details move with the leaf. |
| Human clearance | Ordinary entrances fit an adult at the same perspective depth. Human crawlways fit a crawling adult rather than reading as animal holes. Intentional tiny openings, such as the Tea Room's four-inch hole, retain the scale required by their puzzle. |
| Drop, take, and move | Take an item from its original support, carry it across a transition, and drop it here. It disappears when taken and reappears on a sensible surface. If the story puts it in a boat, into a container, or out of reach, the result follows that state. Try two or more drops in one room. |
| Valid actions | Exercise each distinct outcome through the normal button path. Verify the response, score, inventory, location, and resulting art where they apply. Return through the route when passage is two-way. |
| Rejected actions and hazards | Try a wrong tool, blocked exit, closed container, wrong order, missing prerequisite, or timeout when the scene supports it. Confirm the resulting refusal, damage, death, or recovery. Leave behind a useful item when possible and verify that its fate follows the story. |
| Visibility and focus | Read it at 240 × 160. Cycle focus, open the action menu and target picker, and compare with the default room view. Important silhouettes, openings, markers, and status cues stay visible. |
| UI access | Reach every in-scope object and character using focus and Nearby objects. Open their actions, scroll through the full list, choose a valid target or tool, and cancel back to the same selection. Press Up to open Paths; confirm it lists room exits, puzzle-grid moves, and special story routes without omitting any. In Zork III's Royal Puzzle, survey the map and confirm wall-blocked compass moves are hidden. Include puzzle-dependent exits and verify their blocked and unlocked outcomes. Repeat with unread story pages and with lists longer than one screen. Check that blocked outcomes come from the story and that the UI still provides a route to any available action. See [route-state-audit.md](../docs/audits/route-state-audit.md) for sampled route states and known special cases. |
| Text and controls | Names, prompts, and responses fit or paginate. In a room, Up opens Paths; within Paths, the D-pad selects and A tries the direction. A, B, L/R, Select, and Start perform the displayed action. Back returns to the expected selection without spending a story turn. |
| Persistence | Change the room or its contents, leave by an available exit, and come back. Verify object location, inventory, artwork, exits, and puzzle state. Use the cartridge's Save menu, make another change, then Restore and check the earlier snapshot. Where relevant, also return to the launcher or reopen the cartridge and Continue. Account for any state the story legitimately changes while you travel. Randomly changing descriptions select art from the text actually printed, preserve that appearance on Restore, and consume no extra story turns or random draws. |
| Timing | Check pending story text, light, enemy turns, and timed hazards. Browsing reads or menus shouldn't advance the story. |

For interface screens such as the launcher, bag, map, pause menu, speech picker, and keyboard, apply the same checklist to what the screen promises. Include empty, long, and scrolled lists where relevant. For death and the ending, verify pagination, what the player can do next, and the destination.

Record **Pass**, **Issue**, **Unchecked**, or **N/A** for each applicable check. An asset review can establish perspective or grounding. A replay can establish story outcomes. A native emulator capture can establish how they read together.

Run `make test-button-screens` for the controller sweep. It loads the gallery’s
story checkpoints, walks route-only entries through Paths, and replays the
Royal Puzzle Restore and the Zork III ending. In each explorable state it checks
menu navigation and cancellation without spending a turn, then selects **Look**
and examines every **Nearby objects** target. Each examination starts from the
same room checkpoint, with any route to the room replayed before selecting the
target. A missing Examine option fails the sweep. The per-screen summary in
`build/button-screen-results.tsv` records coverage, and
`build/button-examine-results.tsv` records each command, destination room, and
whether the story returned text. The sweep also reloads the room and selects one
applicable action from the target’s primary menu, such as **Read**, **Open**,
**Close**, **Switch on**, **Press**, or **Take**. The separate
`build/button-object-action-results.tsv` report records the story reply and
changes to the room, score, item location, and relevant flags. Refusals are
recorded as story outcomes. The host sweep exercises the cartridge’s button
handlers. Use native captures for the visual checks above.

Before generating scenery, apply the [scenery rules](../assets/SCENERY_RULES.md). Review a small connected set at the actual game crop before expanding generation; correct failed architecture before integration.
